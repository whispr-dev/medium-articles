The Machete Workflow: Art Through Subtraction
In the context of hyperpop and A.G. Cook’s work, subtractive production (also known as subtractive arrangement) is a workflow that prioritises creating structure by removing elements from a dense, pre-existing block of sound rather than building it up layer by layer
1,2
.
The "Machete" Workflow
While traditional analogue recording is typically additive—beginning with a basic rhythm section and continuously overdubbing instruments—digital production in a DAW often leans toward the opposite
1−3
. A.G. Cook's specific subtractive method involves:
Creating Dense Blocks: Duplicating massive MIDI loops and audio stems (stacked supersaws, arpeggios, vocal chops, and percussion) across the entire several-minute length of an arrangement
4,5
.
The Deletion Phase: Using a "metaphorical machete" to construct the song’s structure by muting regions or deleting blocks of MIDI
4−6
.
Dynamic Space: This process results in tracks punctuated by abrupt silences and clean, empty spaces
1,5
. By dropping elements out instantly without warning, the producer creates sudden pivots that keep the listener disoriented or "wrong-footed"
5,7
.
Strategic Use of Mutes
Instead of using complex transitional sound effects like white noise sweeps or risers every eight bars, this style uses mutes as arrangement tools
5
. This allows the listener’s ear to "reset" during a moment of silence before the full, dense instrumentation slams back into the mix
5
. This technique helps manage high-density productions, ensuring they do not become an exhausting "wall of sound"
2,5
.
The Psychology of Subtraction
This methodology requires significant creative confidence
4,6
. In the analogue era, erasing or working subtractively was slow and expensive, but digital DAWs make it fast and reversible via "Undo"
4
. Despite the technical ease, many beginners struggle with the "emotional strength" required to assertiveley use the delete button on work they have already completed
4,6
.
Distinction from Subtractive Synthesis
It is important to distinguish this arrangement style from subtractive synthesis, which is a sound design method
8
. In subtractive synthesis, a producer starts with a harmonically rich waveform (like a sawtooth) and "subtracts" frequencies using a filter to shape the final timbre
8,9
. While both concepts involve removal, subtractive arrangement refers specifically to the architectural structure of the entire song
2,5

9,10

Mastering Techniques for Hyperpop and Bubblegum Bass
In hyperpop and bubblegum bass production, master bus processing is designed to achieve extreme commercial loudness while preserving the sharp, tactile transients of synthetic percussion
1,2
. Rather than relying on a single limiter, which can cause audible pumping and "soften" the mix, producers use a technique known as staged clipping
1,3,4
.
1. Staged Clipping vs. Limiting
The primary goal of the master chain is "controlled damage"—pushing the signal to a high-saturation extreme without losing the central hook or rhythmic impact
5,6
.
The Workflow: Producers typically insert a clipper before the final limiter
7−9
. The clipper truncates the loudest transient peaks (often the kick and snare) by 1.5 to 3 dB
4,9
.
Harmonic Benefit: Because clipping chops off the peak of the waveform instantly, it generates even and odd harmonics
4
. In hyperpop, this added distortion is a stylistic advantage, providing the characteristic digital grit and brightness
4
.
Reducing Limiter Load: By shaving off these peaks first, the final limiter has less "work" to do, allowing it to raise the overall level without reacting excessively or pumping
3,9
.
2. Standard Master Bus Chain
A typical hyperpop master bus chain follows this structural order
9,10
:
Corrective EQ: Used for surgical frequency carving and ensuring the low-end is solid
9,10
.
Multiband Compression: Often applying only 1–2 dB of gain reduction to glue the mix and manage dynamics
10,11
.
Precision Clipper: Plugins like SIR Audio Tools StandardCLIP, Kazrog KClip, or Schwabe Digital Gold Clip are industry standards
9,12,13
.
Final Limiter: A high-quality brickwall limiter (such as iZotope Ozone Maximizer in IRC IV/Modern mode or FabFilter Pro-L2) finishes the chain
9,10,14
.
3. Key Settings and Targets
To compete with professional releases like those from A.G. Cook or SOPHIE, the master must meet specific technical benchmarks:
Oversampling: High oversampling (often 16x) is critical on clippers to prevent "foldback aliasing," which causes harsh, plastic-sounding high-frequency buildup
9
.
Loudness: The target is often an ultra-loud -6 to -5 LUFS for high-energy sections, or -8 to -7 LUFS for streaming-focused mixes
10,15,16
.
True Peak Ceiling: The limiter ceiling is usually set to -1.0 dBTP to prevent inter-sample clipping when the audio is compressed for streaming codecs
9,10
.
4. Hierarchy and Brightness Automation
Instead of just setting a static master chain, hyperpop producers often automate "hierarchy" into the bus
6,17
.
Music Bus Ducking: The instrumental bus may be pulled down by 0.7 to 1.2 dB during the first word of a hook to ensure the lead vocal lands clearly
17
.
Brightness Contrast: Rather than adding noise sweeps, producers may automate a high-shelf EQ boost (e.g., +2 dB at 7 kHz) on the music bus specifically during the chorus to create an immediate sense of energy
17
.
5. Final Quality Checks
Producers emphasize that the loudness ceiling is less important than what survives the playback medium. A common practice is checking the master on phone speakers; if the hook rhythm or vocal intelligibility collapses at that volume, the master is considered unfinished
5,18,19
.

Hyperpop Sidechaining: The Art of Rhythmic Pumping and Clarity
In the landscape of hyperpop and pop production, sidechain compression is a fundamental tool used to create clarity, physical space, and a characteristic rhythmic "pumping" effect
1,2
. It functions by automatically lowering the volume of one audio track (the target) whenever another audio track (the trigger) plays
1,3
.
Core Functions in Hyperpop
Producers like A.G. Cook and SOPHIE use sidechaining not just for technical correction, but as a deliberate stylistic choice to achieve a "glossy" and high-impact sound
4,5
.
Creating "Huge" Impact: In tracks by SOPHIE, sidechaining is cited as the secret to massive bass hits; by ducking the rest of the mix when the bass or kick triggers, the low-end sounds significantly larger and more impactful
6,7
.
Vocal Prominence: It is frequently used to ensure the lead vocal remains "upfront" by ducking background elements like pads, strings, or even the entire instrumental bus during key hook lines
2,8
.
Rhythmic Movement: Heavy sidechaining creates the "pumping" surface common in bubblegum bass and trance, where the sound feels as though it is "breathing" or inflating in time with the kick drum
4,9,10
.
Managing the Low-End
One of the most critical applications of sidechaining is resolving the "fight" for space between the kick and the bass
11,12
.
The Workflow: A compressor is placed on the bass track with the kick drum routed to its sidechain input
13,14
. This ensures the bass ducks instantly when the kick hits, preventing a blurry or muddy low-end
11,12
.
Technical Settings: For a professional hyperpop sound, ducking is typically set for 3 to 6 dB of gain reduction with a 0 ms attack
11,15
. The release should be precisely timed to the song’s rhythm, often synced to a 1/16 or dotted 1/16 note
11,15
.
Advanced and Creative Applications
Beyond standard volume ducking, hyperpop producers employ several advanced sidechain techniques:
Multiband Sidechaining: This allows a producer to duck only specific frequency ranges
8
. For example, a producer might sidechain the bass to the kick only in the sub-frequencies (below 100 Hz), leaving the mid-range harmonics of the bass untouched to maintain its character
8,15
.
Sidechaining Foley: Mutated foley textures and "found sound" percussion are often sidechained to the primary kick and snare to act as high-frequency transient layers, adding an organic but controlled texture to synthetic drums
16
.
Spectral Ducking (Trackspacer): Plugins like Trackspacer are often used as more transparent alternatives to traditional sidechaining
17
. Instead of lowering the overall volume, these tools use a 32-band EQ to carve out the specific frequencies of the trigger track from the target track in real-time
17,18
.
Volume Shapers: Some producers prefer using volume shapers (like LFO Tool or Kickstart) to create sidechain effects
13
. These provide custom automation patterns that offer more precision and flexibility than a traditional compressor
13
.

The Architecture of Bubblegum Bass Percussion
Bubblegum bass drums are defined by a hyper-glossy, synthetic, and highly tactile aesthetic that favors physical-sounding textures over traditional acoustic samples

3. Kick and Bass Management
In a dense bubblegum bass or hyperpop mix, the kick must have a readable attack pattern to stay legible
11,12
.
Sub Ownership: To avoid a blurry low-end, producers choose one dominant "sub-owner" per section
13,14
.
Bass-Dominant Model: The bass owns the sub frequencies (40–80 Hz), and the kick is shortened to a high-pitched "transient click" (2.5–5 kHz) with a very short tail (80–140 ms)
13,14
.
Kick-Dominant Model: The kick provides the sub-impact (45–70 Hz), while the bass is saturated to occupy only the low-mid frequencies (90–180 Hz)
14
.
4. Essential Mixing Techniques
Sidechaining: This is considered the "secret" to making drums feel massive
15
. By ducking the rest of the mix whenever the kick or bass hits, the percussion gains enormous perceived impact
16−18
.
Brightness Automation: Producers may automate a high-shelf EQ boost on the drum bus or the entire music bus during hooks to add energy without filling the track with white noise
19
.
Staged Clipping: Instead of over-limiting, soft or hard clippers (like StandardCLIP) are used on the drum bus or master bus to shave off transient peaks
20,21
. This adds the characteristic digital grit while allowing the drums to remain loud and punchy
22,23
.
5. Recommended Tools and Resources
Plugins: Kick 2 is popular for its precise visual editing of pitch envelopes; Serum and Operator are staples for FM drum synthesis
24−26
.
Sample Packs: For those not synthesizing from scratch, the SOPHIE Splice pack and kits by umru or IGLOOGHOST are industry standards for this sound

The Metallic Architecture of Hyperpop FM Synthesis
Frequency Modulation (FM) synthesis is a core component of the hyperpop and bubblegum bass sound, prized for its ability to generate the sharp, clinical, and metallic timbres that define the genre
1−3
. While often considered more difficult to master than subtractive synthesis, it is essential for creating the sophisticated sound design associated with artists like SOPHIE and A.G. Cook
4,5
.
1. The "Poke Bass" Technique
One of the most practical applications of FM synthesis is the "Poke Bass." This technique uses the blisteringly fast attack of an FM synth to provide a percussive "click" or "poke" at the start of a note
6,7
.
The Setup: A producer programs a patch on an FM synth (like the Arturia DX7 V or Dexed) with a coarse tuning ratio that creates bright harmonics
8
.
The Envelope: The envelope is set with 0 ms attack, a very fast decay (10–30 ms), and no sustain, collapsing the sound into a dry digital click
8
.
The Result: When layered with a warm analog sub-bass from a synth like the Minimoog, this FM transient ensures the bassline remains punchy and legible even on low-bandwidth consumer speakers
6,8,9
.
2. Synthesizing Metallic Percussion
FM synthesis is used to create tactile percussion that sounds like physical materials under tension, such as metal pipes or popping bubbles
10
.
Metallic Snares: These are often built using a pitch-swept sine wave as a carrier
11
. A second sine wave oscillator then modulates the phase of the first
11
. By slightly detuning the modulator (e.g., +25 cents), the producer breaks the integer relationship between the oscillators, creating the dissonant, metallic harmonics characteristic of industrial textures
11,12
.
Static Comb Filtering: The resulting FM sound is often sent through an ultra-fast delay (1–15 ms) with high feedback to create a static comb filter, which makes the hit sound like a hollow metal object
11,12
.
3. Glassy Bells and "Plastic" Plucks
To generate the hyper-reflective plucks and bells found in bubblegum bass, FM is superior to other methods because it easily produces inharmonic sidebands
3
.
Bell Synthesis: In a synth like Ableton's Operator, multiple sine wave operators are stacked
13
. Modulating a carrier with multiple modulators at high ratios creates complex, shimmering bell tones
13
.
Plastic Plucks: For a "plastic" sound, a modulator might be set to a coarse ratio of 3.0 or 4.0 with a decay slightly faster than the carrier
3
. This causes the high-frequency harmonics to damp quickly, simulating the sound of a struck piece of hard acrylic or plastic
3
.
4. Essential FM Tools
Yamaha DX7 (and emulations): The original 6-operator FM synth is a staple for gritty, nostalgic tones and percussive transients
6,14
. Modern software versions include Arturia DX7 V and the free plugin DEXED
15,16
.
Modern FM Matrices: Synths like Xfer Serum and Vital include FM matrices that allow producers to modulate oscillators with various waveforms, providing a more visual and intuitive approach to FM sound design
3,17,18
.
Hardware and Hybrids: Modern hardware like the KORG opsix and Volca FM2 have revitalised the technique for live use and tactile programming
15,16
.
For beginners, the key to FM is experimentation with ratios and envelopes; even small adjustments to a modulator's fine-tuning can radically transform a clean sine wave into a biting, metallic lead
3,12,13
.

The Hyperpop Bass Synthesis Handbook
Hyperpop bass synthesis is defined by a hybrid approach that balances deep, physical weight with sharp, digital "controlled damage"
1,2
. Producers in this genre, most notably A.G. Cook and SOPHIE, frequently move beyond simple presets to create basslines that are both clinical and emotionally resonant
3,4
.
1. The "Poke Bass" Technique
A foundational strategy for achieving a bass sound that is both heavy and legible on small speakers is the "Poke Bass"
5,6
. Developed by David Gamson and utilized extensively in A.G. Cook's productions (such as on Hannah Diamond's Perfect Picture), this technique solves the problem of sluggish analog attacks
6,7
.
The Low-End Engine: A deep, warm sub-bass is programmed using an analog synth (like a Minimoog or Arturia Mini V) with 100% sustain to provide the physical foundation
8,9
.
The Transient Poke: A secondary patch is created on an FM synth (like the Arturia DX7 or Dexed) with a coarse tuning ratio for bright harmonics
8
. The envelope is set to a 0 ms attack and an extremely fast decay (10–30 ms) with no sustain, collapsing into a dry digital click
8
.
Summation: Layering these ensures the FM click "pokes" through the mix to define the note’s start while the analog synth provides the body
8,10
.
2. Synthesis Archetypes and Tools
Producers often blend disparate synthesis architectures to create complex, genre-defying basslines
4,11
:
FM and Additive Blends: Cook often pairs the gritty, nostalgic FM tones of the Arturia DX7 with the sharp, modern harmonics of NI Razor, which uses additive synthesis to manipulate hundreds of sine waves
3,11
.
Wavetable Pliability: Xfer Serum is frequently used for shimmering, detuned bass pads where LFOs are mapped to filters and effects to create movement that mirrors the "bratty" energy of tracks like "Apple"
12
.
Metallic and Physical Modeling: SOPHIE was known for using comb filters and Frequency Modulation (FM) to give her bass textures a metallic, tactile quality
13,14
. Her "bubblegum bell bass" was often achieved by stacking operators in a synth like Ableton Operator with high ratios and fast decays
15
.
3. Arrangement and "Sub Ownership"
In a dense hyperpop mix, managing the relationship between the kick and the bass synth is critical to preventing a "wall of sound" that feels muddy
1,16
.
The Sub-Owner Strategy: Producers must choose one dominant element to "own" the sub-frequencies (40–80 Hz) per section
17,18
.
Bass-Dominant: If the bass synth owns the sub-energy, the kick is shortened to a high-pitched "transient click" (2.5–5 kHz) with a very short tail
18
.
Kick-Dominant: If the kick owns the sub-impact (45–70 Hz), the bass synth is heavily saturated (using tools like FabFilter Saturn 2) to push its harmonics into the low-mid frequencies (90–180 Hz), leaving the sub-range clear for the kick
18
.
Sidechaining for Impact: Aggressive sidechain compression is often cited as the "secret" to making bass hits feel huge
19,20
. By ducking the rest of the mix by 3 to 6 dB whenever the bass hits, the low-end gains massive perceived impact
17,21
.
4. Alternative Layering
Beyond traditional synths, some hyperpop bass sounds utilize mutated organic sources
22,23
. A common technique involves taking a guitar sample in the second octave, applying multiple distortions and EQs, and layering it with a compressed synth pluck processed through a small reverb to add dimension
23
.

Vocal Reshaping and the Art of Formant Shifting
Formant shifting is a digital audio process that modifies the tonal character or "timbre" of a voice without changing its fundamental pitch
1,2
. While pitch shifting alters the frequency of the note being sung, formant shifting adjusts the fixed spectral resonances created by the human vocal tract
3,4
.
Core Technical Definition
What are Formants? In layman's terms, formants represent the "tone colour" of a voice
1
. Technically, they are the broad spectral maximums resulting from the acoustic resonance of the throat and mouth
1,3
.
Independent Control: Traditionally, when you pitch-shift a vocal up, the formants move proportionally, creating the unnatural "chipmunk" effect
3,5
. Formant shifting allows a producer to move these harmonics upward or downward independently of the fundamental frequency
2,3
.
Simulating Physiology: Shifting formants upward simulates a smaller, narrower physical vocal tract, while shifting them downward simulates a larger, more masculine throat
3,6
.
Application in Hyperpop and PC Music
Producers like A.G. Cook and SOPHIE use formant shifting not just for correction, but as a deliberate creative tool to craft "vocoloid" or robotic personas
3,7
.
Synthetic Quality: Shifting formants upward (often described as "helium-infused") creates a bright, hyper-real quality that maintains the original key of the song
3,8
.
A.G. Cook’s Layering Strategy: A signature technique involves blending the lead vocal with two parallel octaves using specific formant offsets to add body without mud
9
:
High Octave: Pitched +12 semitones with the formant dialed down to -3 to add a glassy, metallic sheen
9,10
.
Low Octave: Pitched -12 semitones with the formant shifted up to +4 to provide artificial weight without sounding "demonic"
9,10
.
Essential Tools
Commonly used plugins for this effect include Soundtoys Little AlterBoy, Antares Throat, Polyverse Manipulator, and Celemony Melodyne
3,11−13
. Advanced tools like Antares Throat even allow producers to manually adjust the modeled length and width of the virtual throat and vocal cords for "sci-fi" or "alien" sound design
6,14
.

Vocal Tract Modelling and Synthetic Throat Geometry
Throat geometry customisation is an advanced vocal processing technique that uses physical modelling to reshape the tonal character of a voice by simulating changes to the human vocal tract
1,2
. While standard formant shifting allows for simple upward or downward spectral movement, throat geometry customisation—most notably through the Antares Throat plugin—provides precise control over the modeled physical dimensions of the throat
2,3
.
Core Parameters and Mechanics
The process is centered on adjusting a digital model of the human anatomy to alter timbre independently of pitch
2,4
.
Length and Width: The "Model Throat" section allows producers to manually adjust the length and width of the virtual vocal tract
2
.
Anatomic Logic: Generally, increasing the length and width simulates a masculine throat, resulting in a deeper, more resonant character
2
. Decreasing these dimensions simulates a feminine throat, creating a shorter, thinner, and brighter sound
2,5
.
Graphical Interface: Advanced tools provide a fully adjustable graphic diagram of the throat's geometry in the GUI, allowing for non-linear, custom shaping of the vocal tract's physical structure
2,6
.
Artistic Applications in Production
In the context of hyperpop and the "vocoloid" aesthetic favored by producers like A.G. Cook, this technology is used for both transparent correction and radical sound design
6−8
.
Natural Pitch Correction: When a vocal is pitch-shifted, the formants usually shift with it, creating an unnatural "chipmunk" effect
4
. Throat geometry customisation corrects this by allowing the producer to "reset" the throat dimensions to sound natural even after a significant pitch shift
2,9
.
Character Transformation: Producers can completely transform a singer's identity. For example, a male vocal can be made to sound hauntingly feminine and breathy by shortening the modeled throat length while increasing the width
10
.
"Sci-Fi" and Alien Textures: By pushing the geometry to extreme, non-human values, producers can create "alien" or robotic voices
6
. This is often used to craft the stylized, hyperreal personas seen in PC Music, where the voice acts as a "creative prosthetic"
11,12
.
Synergistic Processing: This technique is frequently paired with other "vocoloid" tools like Antares Mutator for extreme transformations or Oeksound Soothe2 to manage the harsh resonances that can occur when simulating very narrow throat geometries
6,13
.
