tom@whispr.dev needed an identity
so,  I looked to the whispr dev universe, giving user whisprer and the ghostly avatars:

鬼 (ghost/demon)- Japanese kanji
לוחש(whisperer, masculine) - hebrew

and then realized there was scope for taking the alphabetical transliteration rather than th direct translation and seeing what the resulting western characters doing phonetic version of the  foreign language words produced towards the ends of a seemingly western surname:


1. 鬼 (Japanese kanji)

Most common native reading (kun’yomi): oni
Sino-Japanese reading (on’yomi): ki

For the “ghost / demon / ogre” sense people usually mean, oni is the one that carries the weight.


2. לוחש (Hebrew)

Standard romanization: laches (or more precisely loḥesh / lokhesh)
Phonetic approximation in everyday English letters: lo-khesh
(the “kh” / “ḥ” is the guttural sound, like the “ch” in Scottish “loch” or German “Bach”)

Note: לוחש is the masculine form (“whispering / whisperer”).
The feminine is לוחשת → locheshet / loḥeshet.

already results in possible like:

Oni → Oni, Onis, Onier, Onix, etc.
Lochesh / Lokhesh → Loche, Lochesh, Lokesh, Lochez, Lokher, etc.


but after grok did some anagram solver math n stuff he came up with:

Top picks

Holkins
Most natural Western surname feel (sits right next to Hopkins / Hawkins). Still contains the “holk / oni” echo.

Elkins
Real existing English/American surname. Clean, understated, easy to wear. Loses a little more of the exotic flavour but gains instant believability.

Lokeshin
Keeps the strongest “Lokesh” core + a soft Western “-in” ending. Still clearly related to the whisper half without shouting it.

Shelkion
Slightly more unusual, but has a quiet, almost aristocratic ring. The “shelk / ion” still nods to both halves.

Khelison
Retains the guttural “khe” from lokhesh while ending in a familiar “-ison” (like Harrison / Ellison). Acerbic little edge left in it.

Sholkin
Compact, slightly Slavic-tinged Western surname. Short, sharp, memorable.
